FACILITY DEVELOPMENT01 / 09

Building the experiment

With Prof. Narsing Kumar Jha, I helped develop our vortex-ring–droplet facility from material selection and fabrication through optical alignment and calibration. It began with an experimental question, not a ready-made apparatus.

Flow visualisation02 / 09

Seeing a vortex evolve

Fog-seeded images expose the evolving vortex ring. The moving cores provide a controlled setting in which to observe how even small droplets are redistributed.

Flow measurement03 / 09

Measuring the flow

Time-resolved particle image velocimetry (PIV) reveals circulation, vortex-core structure and convection. These measured flow scales set the stage for interpreting droplet response.

02 / RESEARCH SERIES

From droplets to encounters

Tracking the response, finding spatial structure, quantifying near-contact motion.

Vortex–droplet interaction04 / 09

When the vortex meets droplets

Watch how a single vortex passage changes the paths of falling droplets. Near-core encounters and more distant fly-bys tell strikingly different stories.

Lagrangian dynamics05 / 09

Following individual trajectories

Working in the translating vortex frame of reference makes the droplet response easier to see—particularly transverse displacement and the history of each encounter.

Preferential concentration06 / 09

Where do droplets gather?

The droplet field is not random. Voronoï tessellation reveals concentrated groups and depleted regions whose arrangement evolves around the moving vortex.

Pair statistics07 / 09

When close pairs approach

Droplets must both approach and be near each other. Pair concentration and radial relative velocity help quantify kinematic near-contact encounters—not observed coalescence.

APS DFD · ORAL PRESENTATION2026

Vortex-ring-induced clustering and collision enhancement of polydisperse droplets

79th APS Division of Fluid Dynamics Annual Meeting · 22–24 November 2026 · Orange County Convention Center, Orlando, Florida, USA

Presentation date: 24 November 2026 · Particle-Laden Flows: Collision and Aggregation

Read conference abstract +

Cloud droplet growth in warm clouds depends not only on condensation, but also on how droplet inertia and vortical structures organize droplets and promote collisions. To examine this process in a controlled setting, we study polydisperse droplets interacting with an isolated air vortex ring, used as an idealized vortical structure in turbulent clouds. High-speed imaging and particle tracking velocimetry is used to track the droplets in the vortex frame. Their spatial arrangement is quantified using Voronoi tessellation and radial and angular distribution functions, while size- and Stokes-number-resolved relative-velocity statistics describe droplet-pair motion. The measurements reveal a strongly nonuniform droplet field: droplets are depleted from the vortex-core region and accumulate in annular, asymmetric zones around the ring. These inhomogeneities coincide with enhanced radial relative velocities for selected droplet pairs, leading to increased kinematic collision kernels compared with a spatially uniform field. Event-based dynamic collision counts examine how clustering and relative motion contribute to collision efficiency. The results provide a controlled experimental link between vortical transport, preferential concentration, and droplet-collision enhancement relevant to cloud microphysics.

Author affiliations: Gupta Manojkumar Ishwarchandra (Indian Institute of Technology Delhi); Narsing Jha (Department of Applied Mechanics, Indian Institute of Technology Delhi, India).

Funding acknowledgement: This work was supported by the Prime Minister's Research Fellowship (PMRF) and the Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF), Government of India.
