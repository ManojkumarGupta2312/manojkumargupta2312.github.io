DOCTORAL RESEARCH

Droplet dynamics in vortical flows

The work addresses how finite droplet inertia and gravity interact with organised fluid motion to alter droplet trajectories, preferential concentration and collision-relevant pair encounters. Isolated vortex rings provide a controlled setting; rough-wall turbulent boundary layers extend the measurements to sustained shear turbulence.

Motivated in part by the condensation–coalescence bottleneck in warm clouds, with explicit recognition of laboratory–atmosphere differences.

EXPERIMENTS & ANALYSIS

Research

Original experimental images and recordings, arranged in measurement and analysis order.

Vortex-ring experiments

Controlled coherent-vortex dynamics and polydisperse water-droplet response

01 · Experimental facility

Vortex-ring generation and optical arrangement

Vortex rings were generated in a transparent chamber using a controlled pressure-driven arrangement. Laser-sheet illumination, high-speed imaging and tracer seeding provided repeatable measurements of the evolving flow. Water droplets were introduced separately for the interaction experiments.

02 · Flow visualisation

Fog visualisation of vortex-ring propagation

Fog-tracer images reveal the ring's motion and evolving structure. The intensity distribution also permits identification of a tracer-depleted vortex-core region and its surrounding concentration maximum. These observations are distinguished from the subsequent water-droplet interaction measurements.

03 · Carrier-flow characterisation

Vorticity, circulation and vortex scales

TR-PIV was used to quantify the two vortex-core cross-sections and determine circulation, core radius, peak vorticity and convection velocity. Six operating conditions covered circulation-based Reynolds numbers of 10,764–46,160, including nominal laminar and turbulent ring regimes.

04 · Droplet–vortex interaction

Response of polydisperse droplets

The passing vortex produces heterogeneous droplet trajectories. Stronger curvature and transverse displacement occur for many near-core encounters, whereas far-field trajectories are generally less affected. Droplet size, gravity, initial position, vortex strength and interaction history all influence the observed response.

05 · Lagrangian reconstruction

Droplet tracking in the vortex frame of reference

Image-based droplet detection and trajectory reconstruction resolve individual paths and kinematics. Transforming the measurements to the translating, non-rotating vortex frame separates droplet motion relative to the vortex from its overall convection, enabling radial, angular and size-conditioned analysis.

06 · Preferential concentration

Voronoï-based clustering and void formation

Normalised Voronoï-cell areas were compared with matched random Poisson reference fields to quantify non-random droplet organisation. Radial and angular analyses showed spatially non-uniform accumulation around the moving vortex. A representative ring interaction produced an approximately 187% increase in the relative clustering measure from its pre-interaction value.

07 · Droplet-pair statistics

Relative motion and collision-relevant encounters

Near-contact encounter estimates combine the radial distribution function, contact geometry and radial relative velocity. A probability-weighted kernel was approximately 273 times the adopted gravity-only differential-settling reference for one representative case.

This is a model-dependent kinematic estimate from planar tracking—not a direct measurement of physical collision or coalescence.

Turbulent-boundary-layer experiments

Droplet organisation under prolonged turbulent shear

08 · Wind-tunnel facility

An atmospheric-boundary-layer-like turbulent flow

A barrier–spire–roughness arrangement produced a rough-wall turbulent boundary layer of approximately 1 m thickness. Carrier-flow measurements covered nominal free-stream velocities of 1–4 m s−1. The experiment reproduces selected turbulence characteristics of an atmospheric boundary layer, not complete cloud or atmospheric conditions.

09 · Droplet organisation in shear turbulence

Preferential concentration in the boundary layer

Droplets were examined at a free-stream velocity of 3 m s−1 in the outer part of the boundary layer (y/δ ≈ 0.50–0.54). Voronoï analysis revealed persistent non-random organisation after extended exposure to mean shear and turbulent fluctuations. The measured normalised-cell-area spread was approximately 56% above the matched random reference.

APS Division of Fluid Dynamics · Contributed oral presentation

Vortex-ring-induced clustering and collision enhancement of polydisperse droplets

79th Annual Meeting of the APS Division of Fluid Dynamics · 22–24 November 2026

Venue: Orange County Convention Center, Orlando, Florida, USA

Talk: 24 November 2026 (scheduled) · Session: Particle-Laden Flows: Collision and Aggregation

Official APS DFD 2026 programme ↗
Conference abstract

Cloud droplet growth in warm clouds depends not only on condensation, but also on how droplet inertia and vortical structures organize droplets and promote collisions. To examine this process in a controlled setting, we study polydisperse droplets interacting with an isolated air vortex ring, used as an idealized vortical structure in turbulent clouds. High-speed imaging and particle tracking velocimetry is used to track the droplets in the vortex frame. Their spatial arrangement is quantified using Voronoi tessellation and radial and angular distribution functions, while size- and Stokes-number-resolved relative-velocity statistics describe droplet-pair motion. The measurements reveal a strongly nonuniform droplet field: droplets are depleted from the vortex-core region and accumulate in annular, asymmetric zones around the ring. These inhomogeneities coincide with enhanced radial relative velocities for selected droplet pairs, leading to increased kinematic collision kernels compared with a spatially uniform field. Event-based dynamic collision counts examine how clustering and relative motion contribute to collision efficiency. The results provide a controlled experimental link between vortical transport, preferential concentration, and droplet-collision enhancement relevant to cloud microphysics.
